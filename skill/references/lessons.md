# Lessons that shaped the rules

Distilled from one real outcome and a public talk by a designer who landed a staff product design role. No names or private data.

## What lost the role
The posting said US-remote only, no visa sponsorship. The page and cue card were strong; the recruiter called the work impressive and passed on location alone. A page cannot beat a hard rule. Rule 7 (preflight) exists so effort is never spent before the question is asked.

## What the page still lacked (graded against a good case-study structure)
Situation and context, hypothesis, validation signals, vision, how stakeholders were won over, what shipped, what was learned. The first version had design habits but no decision story, no stakeholder story, no way to judge success, no references, and no answer to "is it safe to hire someone in another country?".

## Simplicity
Write as if explaining to a 5-year-old and as if the reader has no idea of the candidate's competence or anything technical. Reading while listening is a conflict: a text-heavy page makes the speaker a narrator of slides. Put the detail in a "read later" layer.

## Risk analysis
Every hiring decision is a risk decision. Name the risk (location, contract, a missing domain) and give the smallest safe first step (a short paid first project), rather than hoping it goes unnoticed.

## Self-scoring rubric (use it to review a finished page)
Eligibility and hiring risk 15, domain match 10, story (problem, guess, decision, outcome) 15, evidence and results 15, stakeholder and risk handling 10, fits the live time 10, craft 10, honesty 5, plan tied to their process 5, references 5. Treat the total as a judgement, not a measurement.
